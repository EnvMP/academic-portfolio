01 / ABOUT

Research grounded in measurement and mechanism.

My work connects transport and reaction engineering, interfacial materials behavior, analytical chemistry, and dependable quantitative models. Applications include sustainable water reuse, PFAS adsorption, oxidative transformation, process monitoring, and resource-efficient separation systems.

I earned a Ph.D. in Environmental Engineering with a minor in Chemical Engineering (University of Arizona, 2016; GPA 4.00/4.00), following an M.S. in Environmental Science and Engineering (GIST, 2011) and a B.S. in Environmental Engineering (Pusan National University, 2009, Chemical Engineering division). Graduate A grades in advanced chemical engineering transport phenomena, thermodynamics, and reaction engineering support my preparation to teach fundamentals.

02 / RESEARCH PROGRAM

Three connected research directions

Past experimental and modeling work provides the foundation; the projects described below are proposed directions for Cal Poly Pomona, not completed projects.

01

Selective adsorption & reactive material interfaces

Established: PFAS breakthrough and adsorption studies using GAC and MIEX, including the roles of molecular and carbon properties. Proposed: measure competitive uptake, regeneration, kinetic limitations, reactive interfaces and genuine contaminant destruction using appropriate chemical balances.

02

Energy- & resource-efficient hybrid separation systems

Established: numerical membrane-transport analysis and experimental pre-ozonation/fouling studies. Proposed: compare membrane-only, membrane–adsorption and membrane–oxidation systems using measured treatment performance, chemical and energy demand, residual generation and cost.

03

Physics-informed sensing, chemometrics & AI

Established: reaction-kinetic surrogate monitoring, online water-quality sensors, high-resolution MS and statistical analysis. Proposed: integrate mechanistic models with machine learning to build uncertainty-aware soft sensors validated under changing water chemistry, sensor drift and unseen operating conditions.

03 / HIGHLIGHTED PUBLICATION

WATER RESEARCH · 2017

Predicting treatment through the chemistry behind the measurements.

My 2017 paper developed indicator and spectroscopic surrogate models from second-order ozone and hydroxyl-radical reaction kinetics. Instead of relying solely on empirical sensor correlations, the models linked observable measurements to contaminant attenuation mechanisms.

The study examined meprobamate, UV absorbance at 254 nm, and total fluorescence, reporting R² above 0.80 across the tested trace organics and wastewater matrices. This published foundation motivates future kinetic–AI models that adapt to changing water chemistry while retaining physical constraints.

Learn by Doing, with evidence and independent reasoning

Teaching practice and proposed pedagogy are deliberately distinguished.

I served as a 2015 teaching assistant for CHEE 582, Emerging Environmental Contaminants at the University of Arizona. As a guest lecturer in CHEE 204, Water and Energy: Conventional and Alternative Systems, I used videos, slides, diagrams and readings to explain forward-osmosis transport; student feedback emphasized the value of visualizing salt and water movement.

My CV also documents guest lectures on trace organics and organic-matter characterization and a GIST summer lecture on MATLAB/COMSOL environmental modeling.

I mentored undergraduate researcher Austin D. Ziska through interpretation of experimental results and scientific writing; this work contributed to a coauthored Chemosphere publication in 2016. Mentorship has included organizing scientific arguments, managing references, preparing figures and distinguishing evidence from interpretation.

PROPOSED TEACHING PRACTICE

Experiments, Python and responsible AI

At CPP, I would connect physical sketches and derivations with measurements, introduce scaffolded Jupyter notebooks in balances, thermodynamics, transport and kinetics, and teach students to test AI-assisted solutions against units, conservation laws, boundary conditions and independent data. Rubrics, low-stakes prerequisite checks and constructive feedback would support students with varied preparation.
